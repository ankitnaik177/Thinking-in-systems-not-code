Field note · Cricket

Bowled by a
late swinger

I got out to a ball that pitched on middle and hit off stump. I simulated the delivery to find out what I could have done differently.

Ankit Naik  ·  Thinking in Systems  ·  August 2026  ·  Download PDF
Summary

Findings

  • Length hardly changes how much an outswinger moves: full, good or short, it swings 0.38 m. Length changes how much of that movement comes after the bounce, which is probably why the swing looks late.
  • Because the swing is a fixed offset, the batter only has to read the starting line and add the offset.
  • The sideways force is aerodynamic and set by pace and seam angle. It does not depend on where the ball bounced.
  • Meet the ball further out by guarding outside the crease or striding to the pitch. Either halves the movement that arrives after the bat is committed.

Closing

Judge the line
and meet it early

I looked for a way to predict the swing and found that it barely varies: it is 0.38 m on almost every ball. What varies is where the bowler aimed it and how much of the drift is still ahead of me when I commit.

  • Read the release and the pitch, then allow about a ball and a half to the off side.
  • Meet it as close to its pitch as possible: guard outside the crease, a full stride or both. That halves the late movement.
  • Don't play across it. The ball is already going the other way.
  • The ball swings the whole way from release; the "late" part is the movement after the bounce.

Method and limits

The numbers here come from one configuration: a new ball at 119 km/h, seam angled 20°, released 2.2 m up. Read the limits before you trust the conclusions.

  • Movement off the seam at the bounce is not modeled. The ball is a smooth sphere, so the pitch gives bounce and friction but cannot grip a raised seam. Seam movement does depend on length, and it could change the result of Chapter 1.
  • The model has aerodynamic swing only: drag, a seam-driven side force and a Magnus force from backspin.
  • The surface state is a single number, set here to a new ball. Reverse swing is in the model but switched off for this study.
  • The model gives the point where the ball is met. Guarding outside the crease and striding to the pitch reach the same point, and the model does not include what standing out costs: being stumped, or giving the bowler a different length to aim at.
  • The side-force coefficients are semi-empirical: chosen to reproduce the magnitudes observed in play, without a fit to measured data.

How it was built

  1. Ball flight and bounce as a Modelica model: multibody dynamics with a contact model for the pitch, solved in Wolfram System Modeler.
  2. Aerodynamics as a custom component: drag, seam side force and Magnus lift, computed from the ball's velocity and spin.
  3. Trajectories exported and rendered in Blender, driven headlessly from the simulated data.
  4. The dotted line in every figure is the same delivery with the side force set to zero.
