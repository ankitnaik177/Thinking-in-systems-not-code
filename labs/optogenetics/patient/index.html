Be the patient

In 2021 a person who had been blind for years could find and count objects on a table again.

Doctors put a light switch from an alga into the nerve cells at the back of the eye. It answers to amber light. The patient also wore goggles: a camera that sees what changes, and a lamp that flashes amber light into the eye.

Here you can try the same tasks and see why it is not normal sight.

Light switch
A protein from an alga. Light opens it, and the nerve cell fires a spike.
Spike
A short electrical pulse a nerve cell sends to the brain.
Goggles
A camera that only sees changes, and an amber lamp that flashes where something changed.

Based on Sahel et al., "Partial recovery of visual function in a blind patient after optogenetic therapy", Nature Medicine 27 (2021) 1223–1229: ChrimsonR in retinal ganglion cells, goggles with a camera that reacts to changes in light and projects amber pulses; with the goggles the patient could perceive, locate, count and touch objects, and without them could not.
