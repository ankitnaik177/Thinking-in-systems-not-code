Light switches behaviour

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (Deisseroth, Hegemann, Nagel) was for a light switch for brain cells. Here you can use that switch yourself: shine light into the brain of a (cartoon) mouse and see what happens.

Neurons: computed live from the equations of the Optogenetics Modelica library. Mouse: a descriptive illustration, not a model of behaviour.

New here? Start with this

  1. Your brain is made of billions of tiny cells called neurons. They talk to each other with tiny electric pulses, called spikes.
  2. In a green pond alga, scientists found a protein that works like a light switch: when blue light shines on it, it opens and lets electric charge flow in.
  3. Put that switch into a few brain cells of a mouse, shine light through a thin glass fibre, and you can make exactly those cells fire. That is called optogenetics.
  4. Try it: pick an experiment below and follow the numbered steps in the yellow Try this box on the left. The steps tick themselves off when you have done them.
No real mouse here. The brain cells are a computer model (the same equations as the Modelica library behind this page); the mouse is a cartoon that shows what the real experiments found.
